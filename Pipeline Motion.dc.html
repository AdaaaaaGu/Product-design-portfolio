People picture a pipeline as a tube. One diameter, one length, one set of numbers to enter.

Real lines change diameter along their length. Every change is another full set of parameters, entered by hand.

And no line runs alone. Each junction starts another line — its own diameters, its own distance profile, nested one level down. This is what the input form had to hold.

Count the places the engineer has to re-enter everything: eleven diameter changes, two junctions. That count — not the table — was the real problem to design against.
